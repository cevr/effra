# North star

Effra is becoming a language for servers that preserves Go-like simplicity and fast native builds, makes Effect-style contracts and owned lifetimes explicit, and adds algebraic data types and useful abstractions in the spirit of Borgo. Straightforward host interop and compiler-backed inspection serve both humans and agents. `.ef` is its source format; `ef` is its command.

The owner reaffirmed this balance on 2026-10-06. Simplicity means a small set of regular concepts, readable local control flow and predictable costs; it does not mean removing abstractions and making every application repeat their implementation. Effect-grade guarantees require executable evidence for typed failures, dependencies, cancellation and completed cleanup, with unsupported and foreign behavior clearly distinguished.

Owner direction added 2026-10-08: strong abstractions and zero-cost implementations
are a joint destination. A higher-level Effra construct must make the illegal
states and invalid operations covered by its checked contract unrepresentable in
checked Effra source, while foreign and trusted behavior remains explicitly
qualified. It must preserve the same validation, ownership, cancellation and
cleanup contracts as an explicit implementation. Its admitted uses should erase
or lower directly enough that abstraction-induced executable size, allocation,
dispatch and residual-check costs are visible and measured against semantically
equivalent explicit Go and TypeScript/Effect baselines. This is an acceptance
obligation, not a claim that current lowering has zero overhead or that every
abstraction can be universally free.

Owner performance direction added 2026-10-08: a compiled native Go application
must meet or exceed an optimized idiomatic Go application on a matched workload
when both preserve the same validation, failure/service rows, ownership,
cancellation and completed cleanup. This is a destination acceptance target, not
a current result. The JS target must pursue every measurable lowering and
specialization opportunity in its admitted hot paths, including static `match`
dispatch to `if`/`switch` forms when the generated result is better. A generated
or specialized effect runtime may be machine-written in a form a human would
not choose, and a many-times speedup may be a useful hypothesis to test, while
the pinned default Effect-compatible JS ABI and userland runtime contract remain
intact. These are measured ambitions, never a universal multiplier or a reason
to weaken errors, scopes, cancellation or completed cleanup.

Additional owner direction, 2026-10-06: state machines are a vital primitive and should be straightforward to express and inspect at language level. Compare Effect Machine and XState before fixing the supported transition/lifecycle contract. Rust-style derivation should remove structural codec boilerplate while retaining Effect-style transformations between distinct wire and domain types, including explicit failures and service requirements in each direction. Compare Gleam, ReScript and TypeScript for host-language compilation and interop. These are implementation requirements under research, not claims of current support.

The owner sharpened the priority on the same date: keep Effra as Go-like as possible—simple and explicit, adding the capabilities Go forgoes. XState v6's active PR/branch is the main machine prior art; v5 is historical comparison only. New primitives reuse ordinary data, functions, effect rows and scopes. Common source should not require a framework vocabulary, a separate schema declaration for every type, or a tuple of type-level bookkeeping parameters just to call a library.

Owner architecture-loop direction, 2026-10-06: actively seek useful primitives that make writing Effect explicit, declarative and delightful. Every pass traces recurring production ceremony, identifies the underlying contract, and compares a small regular primitive against ordinary library composition. Prove adopted improvements with two unrelated runnable callers and negative controls: shorter wiring must retain visible execution, failures, services and owners. Readability and inspectability are acceptance criteria; line-count reduction alone is not evidence. Research sketches remain proposals until they execute on their advertised targets.

Owner layer direction, 2026-10-06: make `layer` a first-class declaration composing `service` operations and `impl` behavior/construction. The language assembles a checked lazy acquisition graph; the runtime owns shared acquisition, rollback and completed cleanup. Infer exposed `provides`, construction `raises` and remaining `uses`; checked annotations may narrow outputs or bound rows. Bindings, rather than graph leaves, determine an unannotated layer's exposed services. Merge by node identity, diagnose ordinary duplicates, replace deliberately before acquisition, and provide once at an execution boundary. Domain functions retain requirements. See [layer contracts](docs/specs/layers.md); this is authorized implementation work, not current layer support.

The owner further clarified that machine steps must be allowed to be effectful. Admit both ordinary pure and effect functions with explicit contracts; serialize their execution under the actor's owner, preserve failure/service rows, and commit state only after successful evaluation and cleanup. Local state commit does not imply rollback of external effects.

Owner machine direction, 2026-10-07 and 2026-10-08: "if we're adding a special compile check for machines, then it should be a language construct rather than just api". `machine` is therefore a visible construct, and it is provider-independent: a construct that any userland runtime can provide. The compiler checks the declaration and lowers it to a provider-independent machine plan. An ordinary userland provider owns execution, admission, scopes, cleanup and observation, and the compiler never dispatches to a built-in machine engine. Effra ships its own userland machine runtime as the first provider; it uses only the public provider boundary that any second provider could use. See the [machine provider contract](docs/specs/machine-provider-contract.md). This is authorized implementation direction, not current provider support.

Owner construct direction, 2026-10-09: "i think in the case of machines, syntax is preferred over special cased library constructs" — "and generally i should say all of our langauge constructs". Whenever the compiler must know a construct's meaning, for checking, inference, plan facts, inspection or special lowering, that construct is syntax: a keyword, operator, declaration form, postfix construct or predeclared language identifier defined by the language (Go's predeclared `len` and `error` model). It is never a bundled or prelude library item that the compiler recognises by identity or name to grant language semantics, which is the Rust "lang item" pattern. Library code the compiler treats exactly like user code stays library; an item becomes library by losing every compiler branch, not by moving its declaration. A library intrinsic, which swaps the implementation of an ordinary declared signature without changing diagnostics, types, rows or ownership, is a separate permitted category and must be documented; so is the implementation of a syntax construct. A language protocol, a row label whose charging a construct's own rule defines (such as `Foreign` for host calls), is language; an ordinary capability service stays library. Foreign identities that `import go` adapts, such as `context.Context`, form that construct's finite host-protocol table. This sharpens the 2026-10-07 [visible-construct refinement](docs/design.md#language-design-principles) and supersedes the earlier plan to turn `.catch` and `.provide` into ordinary library operations: they are dedicated parser forms today, and they stay syntax. The [compiler-known constructs audit](docs/research/compiler-known-constructs-audit.md) inventories current and planned violations and their migrations; it is direction, not a claim that they are fixed.

Owner runtime direction, 2026-10-08: userland should be able to provide a superior effect runtime, and Effra intends to supply one: possibly a generated runtime that no one would write by hand, keeping the same guarantees while running many times faster. The language exposes checked constructs and a lawful runtime contract; a runtime is an ordinary provider of that contract, selected visibly, in the same way JSX notation selects a user-defined pragma instead of a framework adapter. Until a scoped, reviewed provider decision admits another representation, the JS ABI stays pinned and Effect-compatible and pinned Effect remains the default and reference provider. Provider laws and evidence categories live in the [lawful runtime contract](docs/research/lawful-runtime-contract.md). The speedup is a measured ambition under the performance direction above, not a current result.

Small binaries are another owner-established north star, 2026-10-06. Keep unused standard-library and runtime facilities out of application executables, including when a fluent API is used. Library availability in the distribution must remain separate from application retention, while a user-declared foreign Go import is a required package-initialization dependency rather than an unused facility; module selection, initialization and direct-call lowering need measured size and reachability evidence.

Owner interop constraint, 2026-10-06: preserve the standard protocols that make Go libraries compose. Automatically import native type identities, methods and structural interfaces such as readers, writers, closers, filesystems and contexts, while retaining native values and useful optional method sets. Signature compatibility is distinct from resource ownership, cancellation, shared mutation and behavioral trust. The [language/library partition](docs/research/effect-language-boundary.md) keeps checked Effect contracts in regular language mechanisms and facility policies in modules; broader host objects and methods remain implementation work.

Owner data constraint, 2026-10-06: Effra has no `nil` or `null` language value, nullable ordinary type or observable uninitialized field. Absence is an explicit closed alternative, with bundled `Option<T>` supplied by the generic-data contract. Nullable host results adapt at the boundary; a native nil pointer cannot become `Some` of an ordinary host reference. Preserve Go's error and partial-result semantics, including a nonnil error interface with a nil dynamic payload. Native runtime internals and wire protocols may contain null representations without exposing them as an unchecked Effra value. See [absence and host boundaries](docs/specs/absence-and-host-boundaries.md). Wider host adaptation remains implementation work.

Actor direction under consideration, owner question 2026-10-06: compare BEAM/OTP supervision and hot code loading, Effect Cluster, celld, Rivet and Cloudflare Durable Objects. The [research](docs/research/actor-model-and-durability.md) recommends checked local actor ownership and explicit supervision, with durable entities and workflow replay supplied by optional providers/libraries. Preserve native Go builds and exclude unused actor/storage/deployment modules from application retention. Shared mutable host values, process failure, ambiguous external effects and code upgrade boundaries must remain explicit. This comparison does not expand the first machine profile into distributed execution or certify those guarantees; a later finite contract and executable conformance are required.

Owner refinement, 2026-10-06: actors are owned addressable behavior, not necessarily machines. Actor execution primarily belongs to runtime/stdlib and reuses ordinary service/impl/effect/uses/raises/scope contracts. Typed handlers and restricted receive loops are useful without a new declaration. Machines add transitions and entry lifetimes; message-free effects remain fibers. Supervision, durable addressing and persistence compose with either behavior independently. The [actor contract](docs/specs/actors.md) resolves the proposed initial payload/host-alias, acknowledgement and inspection boundaries; syntax must earn a guarantee common mechanisms cannot express. Source review is input, not local/distributed runtime proof.

Syntax naming follows the owner's 2026-10-06 clarification: expected failure rows use `raises`, alongside service `uses` rows. Replace the earlier `throws` keyword consistently in admitted source, examples, diagnostics and tooling documentation. Keep naming idiomatic and regular; this spelling change does not alter typed failures, defects or cancellation semantics.

The canonical no-value contract is `void`: the `void` type and bare `void` expression describe successful completion without an information-bearing result. Empty parameter and argument lists remain `()`, while `()` is rejected as a type or expression so no-value syntax cannot be confused with grouping or calls. `void` is not absence, `never`, nil, null, or a backend sentinel; `Option<void>` retains its `Some`/`None` distinction on both targets. The Go target may use a zero-size carrier in value positions, and JavaScript may use `undefined`, but those representations do not change the source contract.

Tooling must expose editor-grade diagnostics and full types through the CLI and MCP as well as the language server (owner, 2026-10-06). Errors, warnings and advice retain their severity, source ranges and checked revision across surfaces. Full types include data structure, callable contracts, dependencies and available ownership evidence; a display name alone is insufficient. Bounded references preserve complete type identities without recursively expanding entire programs. See the [shared tooling contract](docs/specs/semantic-tooling.md); this is a requirement, not a claim that the editor adapter is implemented.

The owner also requires user-defined lint rules (2026-10-06). Ship a versioned, inspectable extension API over the same compiler facts, with configurable rule packs, meaningful source-based tests and shared CLI/MCP/LSP reporting. Project policy may add advice or fail a lint gate; it cannot suppress compiler correctness diagnostics or alter checked types. Optional rule execution must not enlarge application binaries or become a mandatory build dependency. See [custom lint rules](docs/specs/custom-lint.md).

The owner requires a built-in formatter (2026-10-06). One canonical `.ef` style and shared printer serve `ef fmt`, CI checks, MCP previews and editor formatting. Preserve comments and directive meaning; formatting needs syntax, not successful typechecking or backend execution. See the [formatting contract](docs/specs/formatting.md). The pure formatter and shared traversal are integrated at `45b43d2`; CLI/MCP adapters are integrated and exercised through real processes, and LSP document formatting uses the same formatter over versioned buffers, pending independent review.

Established 2026-10-05 from the owner's conversation, [design sketch](docs/design.md), [interop direction](docs/interop.md), and the checked prototype. Receipts below refer to baseline `4c497ac`; they identify evidence and gaps, not permanent line numbers. Wider language capabilities remain proposals until a runnable example and a guard establish them.

Current implementation receipt (2026-10-05; managed runtime foundation `5c8c31f`, import/lifecycle update `8b092fc`): `runtime/effra` and `examples/lifecycle.ef` establish managed Go ownership, cooperative cancellation, cleanup, and bounded current-scope inspection. `examples/go-interop` establishes the native Go adapter seam, retaining partial results; primitive Go package functions now import automatically with explicit Foreign capability and reviewed behavior metadata. CLI/MCP inspection still describes a single source file, and shared Go/JS lifecycle conformance covers ownership, unobserved failures and timeout cleanup defects; complete provider parity remains open. See [runtime contracts](docs/runtime.md).

Production foundation receipt (2026-10-05; reviewed integration `e5777f7`): records, payload enums, exhaustive interpretation, typed failure payloads and canonical declaration inspection execute on Go/JS. Reasoned lint suppression cannot disable compiler diagnostics. Provider construction checks configuration and captures dependency values while preserving the invocation owner; recipe/materialization origins and incoming dependents share the compiler graph. Full gate and Go race checks passed. Causal scheduling and bounded ownership checking remain under implementation review, and matched server performance remains unmeasured.

## Tiebreaks

- **Structural correctness** beats **fast move-in-place** (owner AGENTS.md, 2026-10-05): deliver coherent changes in compiling units; reject shortcuts that hide ownership or duplicate authoritative semantics.
- **Explicit contracts** beat **terse syntax** (drafted 2026-10-05): reject shorter syntax that hides a requirement, target restriction, or foreign trust assertion.
- **Truthful target limits** beat **feature parity** (drafted 2026-10-05): diagnose unsupported operations until an adapter proves their contract.
- **Completed owned cleanup** beats **deadline punctuality** (drafted 2026-10-05): a managed timeout may exceed its deadline while waiting for cooperative shutdown; early return requires an explicit surviving owner.
- **A small regular language with deep libraries** beats **one-to-one API syntax** (owner direction applied 2026-10-06): adopt shared type/function/module mechanisms before adding a special compiler operation for each Effect combinator. Shorter source is useful when control flow and contracts stay visible.
- **Measured implementation evidence** beats **familiar API names** (owner direction applied 2026-10-06): upstream tests guide behavior, but copied tests and similarly named functions cannot establish parity. Preserve slower results and unsupported cases in receipts.
- **Strong abstractions with visible zero-cost obligations** beat **repeated hand-written ceremony** (owner direction applied 2026-10-08): compare the same validation, ownership, cancellation and cleanup in an Effra candidate, an optimized idiomatic Go control and a TypeScript/Effect control. Erasure or direct lowering is valuable only when residual allocation, dispatch, checks, retention, executable size and runtime cost are measured; a failed or unmeasured candidate remains unresolved.
- **Contract guardrails** beat **zero-cost slogans** (owner direction applied 2026-10-08): never remove a check, row, owner or cleanup step to make an abstraction appear free. Rust iterator examples, C++ Per.7 design-for-optimization, Per.11 compile-time lowering, P.9 safety-cost and Per.6 measurement guidance, and Go escape analysis are primary comparisons, not universal Effra proof.
- **Measured target parity** beats **a convenient weak baseline** (owner direction applied 2026-10-08): native Go uses optimized idiomatic Go with the same contract; JS investigates static match lowering, direct dispatch and generated/specialized effect runtime paths under the pinned ABI. A possible many-times speedup is a testable ambition, and losing candidates remain visible.
- **One inspectable contract** beats **duplicated declarations** (owner direction applied 2026-10-06): derive structural codecs from checked data and project machine graphs from checked transitions; retain explicit representation, transformation and lifecycle policies. Automatic derivation does not prove a transformation reversible, host data valid, or a machine durable.
- **Go-like local readability** beats **framework API fidelity** (owner clarification, 2026-10-06): use ordinary statements, typed functions and explicit execution for behavior; keep declarative additions small and inspection rich. Adopt proven guarantees without reproducing every upstream combinator, builder or configuration concept in source syntax.
- **Go idioms** beat **novel notation** as the default lens (owner, 2026-10-08: "go idioms are key in terms of simple yet expressive"): start from the Go spelling and shape, and depart only when a recorded decision shows the departure serves a north star better. The owner may override a Go spelling decision by decision; explicit `pub` replacing Go's capitalized exports is one such override.
- **Reconsidering a prior** beats **consistency with it** when the change better serves these north stars (owner, 2026-10-08: "always reconsider priors if it better serves our north stars"): a settled spelling, admission or design reopens on new evidence; record the superseded choice and the reason.
- **Syntax for compiler-known constructs** beats **special-cased library items** (owner direction 2026-10-09: "syntax is preferred over special cased library constructs ... all of our langauge constructs"): first try to make an item ordinary library through a general mechanism. Where the compiler must still know its meaning, spell it as syntax or a predeclared language identifier. Ordinary library stays library, including capability services. A library intrinsic may swap an implementation without changing checking and is documented. The failure mode is a bundled type, method or service that the checker recognises by identity to grant it language semantics, for example `.catch` moved into an `effra/effect` method the checker still special-cases, or a machine rule keyed on a bundled `Step` enum. This refines **A small regular language with deep libraries**: it decides how compiler knowledge is spelled, not whether to add it.
- **Capabilities as layers** beat **compiler-builtin providers** (owner, 2026-10-08: "layers define the impl so http is just an impl of a servicve layer"): HTTP, files, clocks and other capabilities are ordinary services whose implementations a layer provides; the compiler checks rows and ownership but does not hard-code a provider.

- Build for servers. Kernels, hard real-time, and freestanding/no-GC execution are outside the destination (owner, 2026-10-05).
- Preserve unrelated work and use isolated Rifts. Implementers never push. The orchestrator integrates accepted, reviewed and gated heads into local `main` and may fast-forward `origin/main` to them (owner, 2026-10-07: "as these things come in we can push straight to main"). - Run the full repository gate between logical units and before handoff. Performance claims require measured evidence (repository AGENTS.md).
- Keep Wayfinder's HITL decisions open until owner feedback; artifact completion is not decision closure (repository AGENTS.md).
- Keep `.ef` text authoritative and CLI/MCP on the compiler's semantic model (repository AGENTS.md; architecture direction, drafted 2026-10-05).
- Local fixtures and scratch artifacts are the default for live checks. Paid services, deployment, and production data are outside routine checks (drafted 2026-10-05).
- Continue the authorized Wayfinder and architecture-loop work autonomously, extending finite implementation specs with evidence-backed ergonomic, guarantee and performance improvements (owner, 2026-10-06). Follow the standing principles before a genuine question; reversible implementation choices do not require a checkpoint.
- Use pinned Effect source and its licensed test corpus, plus real usage, to guide the bundled library (owner, 2026-10-06). Public examples and application-pattern notes stay generic. Agent roles, models and independent review are set only in [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md).
- Compare Elixir (with Erlang/OTP) and MoonBit alongside Go, TypeScript/Effect, Gleam and ReScript in every language decision where they have a relevant construct; [PRIOR_ARTS.md](PRIOR_ARTS.md#standing-comparison-languages-elixir-with-erlangotp-and-moonbit-2026-10-09) holds the pinned rows (owner, 2026-10-07, 2026-10-08 and 2026-10-09).
